## 3. Decisions and rationale

1. **No fetcher.**
   - jobs.js reads the newest local snapshots (`MELON_SNAPSHOT_DIR`, `MELON_BETA_SNAPSHOT_DIR`) and ledgers (`MELON_HISTORY_DIR`, `MELON_BETA_HISTORY_DIR`). monthly-refresh.yml restores the trusted artifacts first.
   - Each board goes through exactly what `scripts/build-static.js#readSnapshot` does, by importing the same functions (nothing is copied): `rekeyBoardJobs` and `relocateJobs` when `normalizerVersion` is old, then `vetSalaries`, then `scanCompany` (the CI gate's flags), then `attachJuiceAll` and `annotate`.
   - Descriptions are dropped after the scan. Loading is cached per run, so policy.js reuses it.
2. **Ids** are the lead's list plus four new ones it accepted: `jobs-aggregates`, `job-juice`, `insights-distributions` and `market-comps-policy`. `runtime-versions` was added at the lead's request.
   - jobs.js covers 15 ids and policy.js covers 10. `insights-distributions` is a generic pipeline entry; it is computed in the browser.
   - Daily entries carry the updater `pipeline: …; monthly check scripts/refresh/jobs.js`, so the lead's staleness table exempts them. Their `sourceAsOf` is the newest snapshot date.
3. **Modes and statuses.**
   - Every jobs.js Result is `detect` and none writes a file.
   - `flag` means the data or the pipeline looks broken: a stale snapshot, an unquarantined critical salary, a quarantine jump, a stale live deploy, a ledger gap.
   - `drift` means a model or constant no longer fits the data: LEVEL_INDEX, lexicon coverage, backtest movement, Juice coverage.
   - `stale` means a product-policy group's human review is overdue.
   - `skipped` means there was no input (no snapshots or no ledger). An `ok` is never reported for something that was not checked.
4. **Last month.** jobs.js reads the newest `reference/jobs-aggregates/<date>[~n].json` older than this run's data, from `MELON_ARCHIVE_DIR` (default `tmp/archive`).
   - The lead's workflow checks out the data-archive branch there. Without it, the summaries say "absolute bounds only".
5. **Bounds** (`BOUNDS` in jobs.js, `POLICY_BOUNDS` in policy.js). Each one, and why:
   - Snapshot age over **3 days** flags. snapshot.yml and pages.yml each run daily, so 3 days means at least two missed runs.
   - Job count falling by more than **50%** month over month flags. This is the same rule as the archive's `SUSPECT_DROP_SHARE`, and boards do not halve in a month.
   - Pay share falling **15+ points** flags. Pay transparency does not regress that fast; such a drop means a parser or adapter broke.
   - **Quarantine tiers:** a share rising **2+ points and at least doubling** flags; review-only flags use **10+ points**.
     - The gate quarantined 51 of about 4,600 salaried postings on 2026-10-02 (about 1%), so 2 points is a real jump.
     - Review flags are noisy; non-annual intervals alone are about 8% at Anduril, hence the higher bound.
     - More than **10%** of a company's pay quarantined (with 20+ pay-bearing postings) also flags: a tenth of the board's pay is hidden.
   - Any **currency without an FX rate** flags. Those postings silently drop out of comps (build-market `usdMid` / `hasFx`).
   - A company **median moving 15%+** in a month (30+ salaried) flags. Real medians move a few percent; 15% means FX, the parser or the gate changed.
   - **Role families:** more than **2%** of a company's postings without a family (50+ postings), or a rise of **1 point**, is drift.
     - Today 6 of 5,413 postings (0.1%) have none, after the 92/100 hand check.
     - At 2%, 1 in 50 postings vanishes from market comps and the Compare card.
   - **Skill keywords:** more than **15%** of postings without one, or a rise of **5 points**, is drift. Today it is 1.5–8.4% per company.
   - **Seniority default** (the lead's request; see decision 9):
     - Pooled over the stable boards, drift when the share of postings whose level is a default exceeds **50%**. Above that, default "Mid" outnumbers every explicitly levelled posting combined.
     - Also drift when the pooled share rises **5+ points** month over month, or one company's rises **10+ points** (a new title convention).
     - It is 48.8% today.
   - **Unread level tokens** (L5, IC4, Level 3, Grade 2, Band 4) on more than **1%** of a company's postings (at least 3) are drift. Those postings carry a level that `inferSeniority` cannot read.
   - **Juice coverage:** a fall of **10+ points**, or under **50%** of salaried postings (30+ salaried), is drift. Today it is 69% (xAI) to 98%, and the findings list the top unscored cities.
   - **Backtest:** drift when the change exceeds **max(3, 3·SE)** points, where SE of a sample median of |error| ≈ 1.166·m/√n.
     - This is half-normal errors: median 0.674σ, density 0.636/σ there, SE = 0.5/(√n·f(m)).
     - At n = 500 the bound is 3 points; for xAI live (20.5%, n = 126) it is 6.4.
     - Crossing **ACCURACY_LOW_THRESHOLD (25%)** is drift, because the UI then labels the company's estimates "Low confidence".
   - **Live deploy:** the site's `market.json` generatedAt or `meta.compstimate.computedAt` older than **3 days** flags.
   - **Ledger:** last run older than **3 days**, fewer than **0.5 runs/day** since `since`, or a pooled gap over **7 days** between observation dates flags.
   - **Level bands:** at least **3** company × family pairs per level, and a band at least the **median ±5%**.
     - Cell medians of 3–7 postings are noisy, and identical pairs would otherwise give a zero-width band.
6. **LEVEL_INDEX is not a multiplier.** It holds ladder positions (Intern 0 … Director+ 5, Manager 3.5), and `senioritySim` weights comparables by index distance.
   - That weighting is only right if equal index distances mean similar pay distances, so the check reads the ladder as **geometric**:
     - one step = s, fitted as e^median(ln r / Δindex);
     - r = median(level cell) / median(Mid cell), within the same company × family from the market comps doc (so the company's pay level cancels out);
     - level L's implied multiplier is s^(index(L) − 2).
   - Drift when that multiplier is outside the level's IQR band, or when two levels' bands contradict the encoded order. Never auto-written.
   - On 2026-10-02 the step is 1.147×, and all six banded levels are inside their bands:

     | Level | Implied | Data band |
     |---|---:|---|
     | Intern | 0.76 | 0.56–0.77 |
     | Entry | 0.87 | 0.67–0.88 |
     | Senior | 1.15 | 0.99–1.23 |
     | Manager | 1.23 | 1.00–1.30 |
     | Staff+ | 1.32 | 1.10–1.46 |
     | Director+ | 1.51 | 1.43–1.96 |

   - The data places Manager at index about 2.8 (code: 3.5). That is reported, not drift.
   - The demo-data level multipliers (public/features/demo-data.js LEVELS, server/demo.js IC_LEVELS) are compared with the same bands, as information only. Several sit outside them (demo Intern 0.41, Manager 1.34, Staff+ 1.51, demo.js Principal 1.7).
7. **Backtest claims.**
   - The published figure is computed at build from the same `backtest()` and options, so it cannot disagree with the data it was built on.
   - The check re-runs `backtest()` with `server/index.js BACKTEST_OPTS` (seed 20261002, maxN 500, dedupe "role"; read with `extractConst`). It compares the result with three things:
     - (a) the live `api/meta/<slug>.json`, which catches a stale deploy or code drift;
     - (b) last month;
     - (c) the 25% threshold.
   - Hand-written "±N% (tested on N …)" figures in README.md and docs/*.md must match some company within the bound.
   - Not checked: dated process logs (docs/process/), docs/DATA_FRESHNESS.md (generated, quotes the others), and "e.g." lines (docs/QA.md:730 is an example).
   - Boards over 6,000 salaried postings are not re-run (O(n·N); the Pages build does it daily), nor is anything once the 4.5-minute time budget is spent.
8. **"Per tier" in vetting health:**
   - critical = the scan's `CRITICAL_FLAGS`, whether quarantined or not;
   - quarantined = the gate set `salaryFlag`;
   - statistical = `stat_outlier`;
   - review = flagged by the scan but neither critical nor quarantined.

   Each is a share of pay-bearing postings, per company.
   - The local 2026-10-02 snapshots flag Cohere at 29.5% quarantined: 39 `hourly_high`, the "$500 home office stipend" read as $500/hr, from before the parser fix. CI snapshots are re-parsed daily.
9. **Seniority default.** The lead and freshness-audit-surface found that `server/keywords.js#inferSeniority` returns "Mid" only as a fallback. As a result, about half the postings are "Mid" because their title has no level word (xAI 73%, OpenAI 69%, Shield AI 18%).
   - So market-comps Mid cells and Compstimate's Mid weighting are mostly defaults.
   - `public/features/roles.js#inferSeniority` returns null for the same titles; it is the query side.
   - jobs.js measures the default share with `seniorityBasis()` when it is exported, and otherwise with inferSeniority's own fallback (identical today).
   - **USER DECISION PENDING**, recorded in the `seniority-ladder` entry notes:
     - **(A) Keep** (current). The default is a reasonable prior: most titles without a level word are mid-level ICs. The monthly check watches the share.
     - **(B) Expose it:** a `seniorityBasis` field on each Job, and label default levels "Mid (no level in title)" in the drawer and Compare. This is a CONTRACT change.
     - **(C) Infer from the years-of-experience ask** when the title has none (docs/strategy/CORPORATE_LADDER_PLAN.md: "1+ yrs" → Entry/Mid 88%, "5+/8+/10+" → Senior/Staff+ 82–90%). This changes many levels and needs validation.
   - `docs/process/patches/freshness-jobs-seniority-default.diff` adds only `seniorityBasis(title) → 'title' | 'default'` to server/keywords.js, with a test. There is no behaviour change, and it enables (B) or (C).
   - The ladder plan's "41% Mid by default" uses a broader level-word list. The two figures measure different things and are not compared.
10. **jobs-aggregates** (format `melon-jobs-aggregates-1`, about 125 KB for 8 boards). The Result carries `archive: { dataset: 'jobs-aggregates', asOf, json }`, with `json` as text. run.js writes it to `<out>/archive/jobs-aggregates/<asOf>.json`, and the archive workflow records it with record.js. Top-level fields:
    - `format, dataset, asOf, generatedAt, basis`;
    - `inputs`: snapshots, with fetchedAt, normalizerVersion, upgraded, jobs and ledger per board; fx.asOf, minN, the backtest options, LEVEL_INDEX, and the cities count;
    - `companies[]`, per company:
      - counts: jobs, salaried, quarantined, remote, payShare, remoteShare;
      - pay: usd {n, p10…p90}, currencies, noFx, converted, intervals, kinds, sources;
      - level and family: seniority mix, midDefault, levelTokens, families;
      - keyword coverage, vet tiers and codes;
      - juice {scored, coverage, score p10/p50/p90, net dist, grades, top unscored}, freshness buckets;
      - shares, backtest.
    - `families[]`: pooled USD distribution per family, with a per-level split;
    - `market.stable` / `market.all`: the market comps doc's columns, cells and companies, plus a summary;
    - `levels`: level/Mid ratio bands;
    - `pooled`: midDefault share;
    - `backtest`: per company;
    - `ledgers`: health per company.

    No posting titles are included; the examples are stripped.
11. **Policy groups (policy.js)** are `manual` Results with a checklist and a review date, plus copy checks and a cheap data check.
    - **The review clock is not lastChecked**, because run.js bumps lastChecked on every `ok` and a manual review would then never come due. It is the registry's `lastReviewed`, else the entry's `asOf`, else the module default.
    - Cadences, and why:

      | Group | Cadence | Why |
      |---|---|---|
      | juice-score-anchors, juice-grades | yearly | Tuned on one day of postings; pay, tax and rent move yearly |
      | juice-confidence-rules | yearly | Plus a monthly data check |
      | salary-vetting-bounds | quarterly | New boards keep arriving; VETTING.md §8 |
      | salary-interval-factors, salary-parser-bounds | yearly | Wage inflation erodes nominal bounds |
      | llm-vet-config | quarterly | Model ids retire |
      | listing-freshness-buckets, market-comps-policy | yearly | |
      | runtime-versions | semiannual | Node EOL every April |

    - Data checks:
      - **juice-score-anchors:** jobs rounding to 100 ≤ 1% (the old curve put 1.7% there); Rind ≤ 5%; p90 score within 80–95 (target 85–90 per LIVABILITY).
      - **juice-grades:** no grade over 60%.
      - **salary-vetting-bounds and salary-parser-bounds:** published pay within 10% of a quarantine bound ≤ 1%.
      - **listing-freshness-buckets:** no bucket over 80%.
      - **market-comps-policy:** exact cells suppressed by minN ≤ 50%.
      - **juice-confidence-rules:** the confidence counts must match the LIVABILITY Guardrails sentence.
      - **Copy checks:** docs/VETTING.md (11 figures), docs/LIVABILITY.md (K, rounded-100 net, grade $), palette.js JUICE_ANCHORS / JUICE_BREAKS, app.js grade tooltips, vet-salaries FACT / UNFACT, review-rules.mjs F, shared.js and vet.js 2080, update-col.js SOURCE_CLASSES, comps.js minN copy, app.js 180-day threshold, demo.js AGE_BUCKETS, and the pages.yml / VETTING.md / lib.js model id.
      - **llm-vet-config** is `detect`: with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, it calls `GET /v1/models/<DEFAULT_MODEL>`, and a 404 is drift.
12. **runtime-versions.**
    - Inputs: package.json `engines.node` (its minimum major) and every workflow `node-version` (the ci.yml matrix resolved).
    - These are compared with the Node release schedule; a version past its EOL flags.
    - Today it flags Node 18 (engines ">=18", EOL 2025-04-30) and Node 20 (ci.yml matrix, EOL 2026-04-30). Node 22 has 202 days left.
